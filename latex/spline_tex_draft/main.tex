\begin{abstract}
We present \emph{Spline-Textures}, a representation that generalises Heat
Kernel Textures (HKTex) from discrete triangular meshes to smooth,
differentiable B-spline surfaces. The key observation is that on a parametric
surface the first fundamental form is available in closed form and is
everywhere differentiable with respect to the control points, so the
anisotropic heat kernels that HKTex approximates on meshes via discrete
Laplacian eigen-decompositions and $k$-nearest-neighbour heat propagation
admit a direct, analytic definition on the parameter domain. Concretely,
each kernel becomes an anisotropic Gaussian whose covariance is the inverse
metric tensor at the kernel centre, eliminating geodesic distance
approximation entirely. Building on a differentiable NURBS backend, our
formulation jointly optimises surface geometry (control points) and
appearance (kernel parameters) from multi-view images through a single
differentiable rendering pipeline, yielding CAD-native smooth surfaces with
high-fidelity intrinsic textures. We position our contribution at the
intersection of three otherwise-disjoint literatures---differentiable CAD,
surface-bound Gaussian splatting, and intrinsic mesh textures---and show
that this intersection has remained empty.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

Three-dimensional Gaussian Splatting (3DGS)~\cite{kerbl3Dgaussians} has
established explicit Gaussian primitives as a dominant representation for
real-time novel-view synthesis. A well-known limitation, however, is that
the underlying geometry is a fuzzy cloud of oriented Gaussians rather than a
clean, editable surface; extracting a usable mesh requires post-hoc
regularisation~\cite{sugar} or binding primitives to a separate
mesh~\cite{omega, meshgs, gaustar}. In all such cases the surface carrier is
a \emph{discrete} triangular mesh: $C^0$ across edges, with no analytic
metric, and disconnected from the CAD boundary representations (B-spline and
NURBS surfaces) that manufacturing pipelines actually consume.

Heat Kernel Textures (HKTex)~\cite{hktex} address the \emph{appearance} side
of a related problem: rather than UV atlases, they represent texture as
anisotropic heat kernels---the geodesic counterparts of Gaussians---defined
intrinsically on a triangular mesh. Kernels live in barycentric coordinates,
are densified and pruned on the surface, and avoid UV seams, distortion, and
wasted atlas space. Their computation, however, depends on a discrete
Laplacian eigen-decomposition and $k$-nearest-neighbour heat propagation,
which are inherently mesh-discrete artefacts.

In this paper we observe that both limitations---the discrete surface carrier
of binding-GS and the discrete metric of HKTex---share a single root cause:
the triangular mesh. We therefore replace it with a smooth, differentiable
B-spline surface and show that the HKTex machinery simplifies rather than
complicates: the first fundamental form
$g = (E,F;F,G)$ is available in closed form from the surface partial
derivatives, so each anisotropic heat kernel reduces to a Gaussian on the
$(u,v)$ domain whose covariance is $g^{-1}$, with no geodesic distance
computation and no eigen-decomposition. Because $g$ is differentiable in the
control points, appearance and geometry are coupled in one differentiable
graph, enabling joint optimisation from multi-view images---something neither
HKTex (fixed mesh) nor binding-GS (mesh, no analytic metric) can do.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item A formulation of anisotropic heat-kernel appearance on
        differentiable B-spline surfaces, where kernel covariance is the
        closed-form inverse metric tensor, eliminating geodesic distance
        approximation (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item A joint geometry--appearance optimisation framework that updates
        B-spline control points and kernel parameters simultaneously from
        multi-view images through a differentiable subdivision-and-splat
        rendering pipeline.
  \item A positioning of this work at an empty intersection of
        differentiable CAD, surface-bound Gaussian splatting, and intrinsic
        mesh textures, supported by a structured survey of adjacent
        literatures (\S\ref{sec:related}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Method}\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Preliminaries: B-spline surfaces}
A tensor-product B-spline surface of degrees $(p,q)$ is
\[
  S(u,v) = \sum_{i}\sum_{j} N_{i,p}(u)\,N_{j,q}(v)\,P_{ij},
  \qquad (u,v)\in[0,1]^2,
\]
with control points $P_{ij}\in\mathbb{R}^3$, basis functions $N_{i,p}$, and
knot vectors. Its partial derivatives $\partial_u S$, $\partial_v S$ are
again B-spline surfaces of lower degree, available in closed form and
differentiable in the control points via automatic
differentiation~\cite{nurbsdiff,diffnurbs}.

\subsection{Metric tensor and heat-kernel covariance}
The first fundamental form at $(u,v)$ is
\[
  g(u,v)=\begin{pmatrix}E&F\\F&G\end{pmatrix},\quad
  E=\partial_uS\!\cdot\!\partial_uS,\;
  F=\partial_uS\!\cdot\!\partial_vS,\;
  G=\partial_vS\!\cdot\!\partial_vS.
\]
An anisotropic heat kernel centred at $(u_0,v_0)$ with diffusion time
$\sigma^2$ takes the parametrix form
\[
  \mathcal{K}(u,v)\propto
  \exp\!\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{4\sigma^2}\,\Delta\vect{u}^{\!\top}\,
    g^{-1}(u_0,v_0)\,\Delta\vect{u}\Bigr),
  \quad \Delta\vect{u}=(u-u_0,\,v-v_0),
\]
optionally modulated by a learnable anisotropy matrix. On a mesh, HKTex must
approximate this quantity via discrete Laplacian spectra and KNN heat
propagation; on a B-spline surface it is closed-form.

\subsection{Rendering}
Ray--spline intersection has no closed form. We adopt a differentiable
subdivision route: control points are adaptively subdivided into a dense
triangular mesh, gradients flow back through the subdivision matrix, and the
existing Mitsuba-based ray renderer is reused. Appearance at a hit point with
parameter $(u,v)$ is $\sum_k \alpha_k\,\mathcal{K}_k(u,v)\,c_k$.

\subsection{Optimisation}
Two stages. (i)~\emph{Fixed geometry}: fit appearance to a textured spline,
directly paralleling HKTex and validating the metric-kernel formulation.
(ii)~\emph{Joint}: optimise control points and kernels jointly from
multi-view images, with area and curvature regularisation and an
appearance--geometry decoupling schedule to prevent the appearance from
absorbing geometric error.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
We presented Spline-Textures, which lifts Heat Kernel Textures from
discrete meshes to differentiable B-spline surfaces, replacing geodesic
distance approximation with closed-form metric-tensor covariance and
enabling joint geometry--appearance optimisation from images. The output is
a CAD-native smooth surface with intrinsic texture, inaccessible to existing
mesh-bound Gaussian or heat-kernel representations.
