\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

Our contribution sits at the confluence of four literatures that have, to
date, evolved in isolation: (i)~differentiable parametric surface modelling
for CAD, (ii)~surface-bound or surface-extracted Gaussian splatting,
(iii)~intrinsic texture representations on manifolds, and
(iv)~Fourier and harmonic representations of closed surfaces. We review
each in turn and identify the empty intersection that motivates this work
(\autoref{tab:landscape}).

% -------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Differentiable Parametric Surface Modelling}\label{sec:rw:diffcad}

\paragraph{Differentiable NURBS and B-splines.}
NURBS-Diff~\cite{nurbsdiff} introduced a differentiable programming module for
NURBS curves and surfaces integrated into PyTorch, demonstrating unsupervised
point-cloud reconstruction and analysis-constrained surface fitting. The
Diff-NURBS library~\cite{diffnurbs} provides a maintained, pure-PyTorch
NURBS implementation with closed-form surface derivatives and normals under
an Apache-2.0 licence, serving as the geometric backbone of our method.
VALVEFIT~\cite{valvefit} applies GPU-accelerated differentiable B-spline
surface fitting to patient-specific heart-valve modelling, while
NURBS-PyTorch~\cite{nurbspytorch} and TorchNurbs~\cite{torchnurbs} are earlier
differentiable NURBS implementations now largely unmaintained. A common
limitation of this entire family is the absence of a rendering pipeline: the
fitting signal is a point cloud or a prescribed loss, never multi-view
images, so the geometric and appearance channels have not been coupled
end-to-end with a differentiable renderer.

\paragraph{Differentiable rendering of parametric surfaces.}
Worchel and Alexa~\cite{worchelalexa2023} (SIGGRAPH Asia~2023) directly address
this gap: they derive differentiable meshing operators that extract a
representative triangle mesh from a continuous parametric surface
(B\'ezier, B-spline, NURBS) in a differentiable way, enabling image-based
inverse graphics on parametric geometry with coarse-to-fine regularisation.
Our rendering pipeline adopts the same differentiable-subdivision principle
(our \texttt{SplineSurface.to\_mesh} bridge), but extends the formulation from
\emph{geometry-only} reconstruction to \emph{joint geometry--appearance}
optimisation: their method optimises control points against silhouette/image
losses with no surface appearance field, whereas we couple the control points
to an intrinsic heat-kernel texture whose covariance is the analytic metric of
the very surface being rendered.

\paragraph{Learning NURBS representations.}
NeuroNURBS~\cite{neuronurbs} learns compact NURBS surface representations for
3D solids directly from B-rep parameters, reducing GPU consumption by
\ensuremath{\sim}87\% over UV-grid approximations and producing smoother
generated surfaces. This line is complementary to ours: NeuroNURBS targets
generative solid modelling from CAD data, not image-based rendering, and could
supply initial B-spline geometry for our joint optimisation.

\paragraph{Differentiable subdivision surfaces.}
As an alternative to parametric splines, differentiable subdivision surface
fitting~\cite{diffsubsurf} fits Loop subdivision surfaces to point clouds via
an implicit moving-least-squares target coupled with a differentiable
rasteriser. Subdivision handles arbitrary topology more gracefully than
tensor-product B-splines, but yields neither a compact control mesh with an
analytic metric nor a CAD-native output. Our choice of B-splines sacrifices
arbitrary topology (we restrict the present work to single-patch CAD-class
objects) in exchange for a closed-form first fundamental form and direct
compatibility with manufacturing pipelines.

\paragraph{CAD reconstruction and reverse engineering.}
A complementary line reconstructs boundary representations (B-reps) from
unstructured input. DualBrep~\cite{dualbrep} (SIGGRAPH~2026) unifies B-rep
geometry and topology in dual SDF/UDF scalar fields and extracts explicit
B-rep models via flow matching from point clouds. A2Z-10M+~\cite{a2z} and
PS-CAD~\cite{pscad} learn CAD modelling sequences from large annotated
datasets. These methods operate on point clouds or modelling commands and do
not employ differentiable rendering of the parametric surface itself; they
are orthogonal to our image-based, rendering-driven formulation and could
supply initial geometry for our joint optimisation.

\paragraph{NURBS splatting in the plane.}
Closest in spirit to our rendering formulation is NURBS
Splatting~\cite{nurbssplatting} (ECCV~2026), which renders planar rational
spline curves as continuous Gaussian fields by sampling Gaussians along the
curve parameter domain, reformulating vector-graphics rendering as a smooth
Gaussian-accumulation process with stable gradients. The framework elegantly
handles rational weights, non-uniform knots, and closed-region filling.
Crucially, however, it is confined to \emph{two-dimensional} planar curves:
there is no 3D surface, no multi-view consistency, and no joint
geometry--appearance optimisation. Our work can be read as the
three-dimensional, image-driven generalisation of this idea to B-spline
\emph{surfaces} carrying intrinsic heat-kernel appearance.

\paragraph{B-spline splatting kernels.}
A closely related but distinct use of B-splines replaces the Gaussian
\emph{kernel} itself in a splatting pipeline. SplineSplat~\cite{splinesplat}
(SIGGRAPH Asia~2025 Technical Communications) represents the radiance field
with B-spline kernels instead of Gaussians, matching 3DGS reconstruction
fidelity while reducing training time by up to 18\%. The view-dependent
splatting-kernel framework of Ding et al.~\cite{viewdepkernels}
(SIGGRAPH~2026) further generalises this idea and explicitly identifies
B-spline kernels~\cite{splinesplat} as a view-independent 2D-kernel family
that lacks cross-view 3D consistency. Crucially, both works keep the
\emph{primitive cloud} representation of 3DGS---the B-spline only replaces the
fall-off shape of each splat---so there is no explicit smooth surface, no
CAD-native output, and no analytic metric tensor. Our work is orthogonal:
we use B-splines as the \emph{geometric carrier} (a smooth surface on which
kernels live), not as a kernel replacement, and the surface's first
fundamental form supplies the kernel covariance in closed form.

\subsection{Surface-Bound Gaussian Splatting}\label{sec:rw:bindinggs}

A substantial body of work tames the geometric ambiguity of 3DGS by binding
Gaussian primitives to an explicit surface. SuGaR~\cite{sugar} regularises
Gaussians to align with the underlying surface and optionally binds them to a
Poisson-reconstructed mesh for joint optimisation and editing.
OMeGa~\cite{omega} jointly optimises an explicit mesh and 2D splats whose
spatial attributes are expressed in the mesh frame, improving indoor-scene
geometry. MeshGS~\cite{meshgs} distinguishes tightly- and loosely-bound
splats to align some Gaussians to geometry while letting others absorb mesh
artefacts. GauSTAR~\cite{gaustar} binds Gaussians to mesh faces for dynamic
surface tracking and reconstruction, unbinding them where topology changes.
Manifold-GS~\cite{manifoldgs} represents surface-like Gaussians as discrete
unoriented varifolds and exports certified open surface patches.
EGG-Fusion~\cite{eggfusion} performs real-time geometry-aware Gaussian-surfel
mapping for SLAM. Earlier foundations include 2D Gaussian
Splatting~\cite{2dgaussians} and geometry-grounded
primitives~\cite{geosplatting}.

A unifying property of \emph{all} the above is the surface carrier: a
\emph{discrete triangular mesh}. The mesh is $C^0$ across edges, possesses no
analytic metric tensor, and is not a CAD-native representation. Consequently,
kernel covariance must be inferred from discrete geometry, and the output
cannot be fed directly to a manufacturing pipeline without reconstruction.
Our formulation replaces the mesh with a differentiable B-spline surface,
yielding a closed-form metric, $C^2$ continuity, and native CAD
compatibility---at the cost of restricting the present scope to
single-patch, CAD-class topology.

\subsection{Intrinsic Texture and Appearance on Manifolds}\label{sec:rw:hktex}

Heat Kernel Textures~\cite{hktex} (ECCV~2026) represent appearance on a
triangular mesh as a set of anisotropic heat kernels---the geodesic
counterparts of Gaussians---defined directly on the surface. Kernel
positions, optimisation, pruning, and densification all operate in
barycentric coordinates, avoiding UV seams, distortion, wasted atlas space,
and uneven texel resolution. The representation is fitted from an existing
texture or from multi-view images and integrates with physically based
rendering.

The computation of HKTex, however, is intrinsically mesh-discrete: it relies
on a discrete Laplacian eigen-decomposition and a $k$-nearest-neighbour heat
propagation to approximate the geodesic quantities that define each kernel.
Our contribution is a strict generalisation: on a B-spline surface the first
fundamental form is available in closed form and is differentiable in the
control points, so each anisotropic heat kernel reduces to a Gaussian on the
$(u,v)$ domain with covariance $g^{-1}$, and the entire eigen-decomposition
and KNN machinery is eliminated. This simultaneously simplifies the
formulation, makes it differentiable with respect to the geometry, and yields
a representation that lives on a smooth rather than a discrete manifold.

Related appearance-on-surface work includes TextureSplat~\cite{texturesplat}
and SVG-IR~\cite{svgir}, which attach per-primitive spatially-varying
materials to Gaussians, and the broader inverse-rendering line of
GS-IR~\cite{gsir}, Relightable~3D~Gaussians~\cite{relightable3dgaussians},
DeferredGS~\cite{deferredgs}, and their successors~\cite{gigs,gusir,rtrgs}.
These methods model appearance as per-primitive attributes or small MLPs
rather than intrinsic manifold kernels, and they remain mesh- or
primitive-bound.

\subsection{Fourier and Harmonic Representations of Closed Surfaces}\label{sec:rw:fourier}

A parallel tradition represents closed surfaces not by control meshes
but by expansions in a global harmonic basis. The choice of basis is
dictated by the topology of the parameter domain, and the family splits
sharply along genus.

\paragraph{Genus-zero surfaces: spherical harmonics.}
For a closed simply-connected (genus-0) surface, the vertex set admits a
bijective, area-preserving map to the unit sphere $S^{2}$, after which
each Cartesian coordinate becomes a spherical function
$x(\theta,\varphi),y(\theta,\varphi),z(\theta,\varphi)$ and is expanded
in spherical harmonics:
$\mathbf{x}(\theta,\varphi)=\sum_{\ell,m}\mathbf{a}_{\ell m}Y_{\ell m}(\theta,\varphi)$,
with coefficients found by least squares. The canonical pipeline is
\textsc{SPHARM}~\cite{shen2009spharm}, widely used in morphometrics;
Sijbers et al.~\cite{sijbers2002fd3d} reach the same expansion via an
inflation algorithm that maps a polygonised surface onto $S^{2}$ before
the harmonic fit, and Kazhdan et al.~\cite{kazhdan2003sh} use the
spherical-harmonic spectrum as a rotation-invariant 3D shape
descriptor. The basis is orthogonal and intrinsically multiscale---low
orders encode global form, high orders encode detail---but it is
restricted to genus~0 and, being globally supported, exhibits Gibbs-type
oscillations at sharp features and offers no local editability.

\paragraph{Beyond the sphere: spheroidal and toroidal bases.}
The parameter domain need not be a sphere. Shaqfa and van
Rees~\cite{shaqfarees2024soh} observe that oblate or prolate particles
are poorly served by a spherical parameterisation and generalise the
basis to \emph{spheroidal} harmonics---separation-of-variables solutions
of Laplace's equation in spheroidal coordinates---with three mapping
strategies (radial, hyperbolic, and a conformalised mean-curvature-flow
preconditioner for non-star-shaped input), consistently outperforming
SPHARM on non-spherical granular data. For genus-1 closed surfaces the
natural domain is the torus $T^{2}=S^{1}\!\times\!S^{1}$ with periodic
parameters $(u,v)\in[0,2\pi)^{2}$, and the coordinate expansion becomes
a two-variable Fourier series
$\mathbf{x}(u,v)=\sum_{k,l}\mathbf{c}_{kl}e^{i(ku+lv)}$; this is the
deformable-Fourier-model formulation of Staib and
Duncan~\cite{staibduncan1996}, which models tori, open, closed, and
tubular surfaces with a small sinusoidal coefficient set. Higher genus
admits no single canonical Euclidean domain, but a compact orientable
surface of genus $g$ is conformally a genus-$g$ Riemann surface on which
harmonic coordinate functions admit a Weierstrass-type representation in
meromorphic 1-forms; Connor~\cite{connor2016harmonic} constructs such
harmonic parametrisations at arbitrary genus. The unifying principle is
that \emph{the genus of the surface selects the harmonic basis}: no
single Fourier family spans closed surfaces of arbitrary topology.

\paragraph{Volumetric and local strategies.}
When a surface parameterisation is unavailable, the closed surface can
be encoded indirectly: Vrani{\'c} and Saupe~\cite{vranisaupe2001dft}
voxelise the enclosed solid and take the 3D discrete Fourier transform
of the volume, using low-frequency coefficients as a
transformation-invariant shape descriptor; Niculescu
et al.~\cite{niculescu2002doubleefd} apply 2D elliptic Fourier
descriptors~\cite{kuhlgiardina1982} per horizontal slice and a second
Fourier transform along $z$, reconstructing the object by inverse
transform. These volumetric routes are simple and FFT-fast but describe
the \emph{solid's spectrum} rather than the surface itself and require
isosurface extraction to recover geometry. A modern local alternative
sidesteps the global-basis limitation by tiling the surface with
per-anchor spherical-harmonic fields: SHARC~\cite{sapoutzoglou2026sharc}
places reference points in the interior volume, ray-casts the visible
distance field from each, and encodes it with a fast spherical harmonic
transform, achieving genus-agnostic reconstruction at state-of-the-art
fidelity and parsimony.

\paragraph{Relation to our representation.}
The entire Fourier/harmonic family is, by construction, a competitor to
the parametric-spline carrier we adopt: both provide a closed-surface
parametrisation, but with complementary trade-offs. Fourier bases are
\emph{global} (any coefficient perturbs the whole surface), which makes
them natural for shape retrieval, spectral regularisation, and
band-limited level-of-detail, but poor for local editing and prone to
oscillations at sharp features. B-spline bases are \emph{locally
supported}, CAD-native, and admit a closed-form first fundamental form,
but are restricted to tensor-product patch topology in the present work.
The two traditions rarely meet in the literature, and the intersection
is salient for us because our heat-kernel appearance already lives in
the spectral domain of the surface Laplacian: had the geometric carrier
also been a harmonic basis, geometry and appearance would share a single
spectral frame. We instead keep the B-spline carrier for its local
controllability and analytic metric, and couple it to the
\emph{heat-kernel} spectrum of that same carrier, recovering a spectral
appearance representation on a locally-supported geometry---a point of
contact between the two families that, to our knowledge, has not been
explored.

\subsection{Positioning}\label{sec:rw:gap}

\autoref{tab:landscape} positions our contribution along three axes---the
geometric \emph{carrier}, the splatting \emph{kernel}, and the
\emph{appearance} model. NURBS Splatting~\cite{nurbssplatting} establishes the
spline-as-Gaussian-field paradigm but only for 2D curves.
SplineSplat~\cite{splinesplat} swaps the Gaussian kernel for a B-spline kernel
in a 3DGS pipeline, yet retains the unstructured primitive cloud as carrier;
Worchel and Alexa~\cite{worchelalexa2023} render parametric surfaces
differentiably but carry no appearance field. Surface-bound Gaussian
splatting~\cite{sugar,omega,gaustar,meshgs} and HKTex~\cite{hktex} bind
kernels to a \emph{discrete} mesh, while DualBrep~\cite{dualbrep} models
B-rep geometry without rendering. The unoccupied cell is a B-spline
\emph{surface} as carrier, with the surface's own analytic metric supplying
the heat-kernel covariance, jointly optimised from multi-view images---this
paper occupies it.
